# Snow Goose Kennels already covers the hard parts

Snow Goose Kennels has already built the part most Lab breeder sites get wrong: a litter page that shows status, countdown, parents, price, deposit and per-puppy availability, generated from real records so it can't silently rot. The gaps are mostly the trust and process content buyers look for, not back-office features. The five biggest are: no public application form, no "last updated" stamp, no how-it-works/FAQ/policies page, health tests with no OFA number or link, and no SEO/meta. Breeder-management software (BreedTools, BreedTracker, BreederBuddy, Breedera) treats heat tracking, puppy weight logs, litter records and health records as baseline. It treats waitlists, applications, contracts, buyer portals and payments as the paid differentiators, and the site already has the waitlist piece. The owner's no-contract practice is the one place where the evidence points the other way: the Labrador Retriever Club (LRC) Code asks for a written contract, and buyer guides treat contracts and take-back terms as marks of a responsible breeder. That is an inference from those documents, not a legal conclusion, and not legal advice. Whether to change it is the owner's decision. The recommendations below are ranked for a non-technical owner working from her phone: the cheapest, highest-trust items first, with payments, portals, e-signatures and pedigrees deferred. **Evidence caveats:** about 11 distinct breeder sites were read, mostly as text only (no visual or mobile judgments), with no UK or field-trial kennel site opened. Several vendor and review pages blocked fetches, and some prices come from aggregator snippets that conflict.

## Breeder sites: stale pages are the norm, and a dated status line stands out

Across the seven small-to-mid US sites in the first audit, the near-universal elements were a litters page, parent pages, a contact page, a health guarantee and a stated deposit. Rare and impressive were per-litter health tables with certificate numbers, status labels, a written reservation process, and a visible update date ([Heatherdowns](https://heatherdowns-labradors.squarespace.com/our-puppies); [Ridgetop](https://sites.google.com/view/ridgetopretrievers/litters)). The second audit of five more sites (two Canadian) found the same pattern: process pages are thin, and "Apply" buttons often lead nowhere useful ([Lucky Labs](https://chocolatelabradorretriever.ca/lab-puppies-for-sale)).

The dominant flaw was staleness. A page titled "Current/Upcoming Litters" held only a March 2023 litter ([Griffin Keep](https://sites.google.com/view/labradorsofgriffinkeep/currentupcoming-litters)). A 2019 litter was marked "SOLD OUT" while still saying "Call now to reserve your pick" ([Ridgetop](https://sites.google.com/view/ridgetopretrievers/litters)). Heatherdowns carried an "Updated as of 11/21/2026" banner and clear status labels, but the same page had impossible whelp/release dates and statuses that contradicted the body text. A dated line helps only if the data under it is right. Because a Wix, Squarespace or Google Sites page needs a manual edit per litter, staleness is structural rather than laziness (my inference; see the builders section). Snow Goose's litter cards are driven by status and dates in the admin, so they avoid this by design.

Health display varied widely. The best example lists OFA hips and elbows with certificate numbers, eye exams, and EIC/CNM status for both parents ([Ridgetop](https://sites.google.com/view/ridgetopretrievers/litters)). Heatherdowns shows a full DNA panel per litter but sends readers to other pages for certificates, with inconsistent abbreviations. Several sites showed no results at all ([Lucky Labs](https://chocolatelabradorretriever.ca/lab-puppies-for-sale); [Ridgemere](https://ridgemere-retrievers-1.showit.site/)). No site opened used badges or direct OFA database links. That makes "OFA number with a link to the record" a differentiator that nobody sampled offers.

Prices were mostly hidden, though deposits were usually stated. Small breeders posted deposits of $400 to $500, non-refundable ([Sunflower Hill](https://sites.google.com/view/sunflower-hill-akc-labs/labrador-puppies/reserving-a-puppy); [Griffin Keep](https://sites.google.com/view/labradorsofgriffinkeep/currentupcoming-litters)). Heatherdowns showed **$3,450** per puppy. Apple Valley gave the clearest money statement: a $50 waitlist deposit, a $500 down payment, the balance due at 7 weeks, and a 2026 price of $2,800 including HST ([Apple Valley](https://sites.google.com/applevalleylabradors.ca/applevalleylabrador/home/puppies/purchase-info)). Applications mostly run through an outside Google Form, Jotform or an email questionnaire, so the site itself has no workflow. Snow Goose already shows price and deposit on each litter, which puts it ahead of most.

Some patterns erode trust. Heatherdowns publishes buyer names with reservation dates, a privacy risk Snow Goose's whitelisting already avoids. It also voids its guarantee unless the buyer purchases specified supplements, food and insurance ([Heatherdowns guarantee](https://heatherdowns-labradors.squarespace.com/health-guarantee)). Free-platform URLs and generic email addresses were common and visible to buyers. The most copyable ideas were a three-step reservation (deposit, questionnaire, signed agreement), a deposit-transfer-between-litters rule ([Wildrose](https://koala-fife-5gaw.squarespace.com/puppies)), a "Retired, Forever Loved" page, buyer-education articles, and a plain-language lifetime-return statement ([Arctic Texan](https://sites.google.com/site/arctictexanlabradors/home); [Twisted Clovers](https://twisted-clovers-labradors.squarespace.com/your-questions)). Gaps in this evidence: photo style, typography and mobile behavior could not be assessed, and testimonial and guarantee pages were mostly not opened.

## Standards and buyer expectations: health proof is expected, and "no contract" reads as a red flag

The most Lab-specific standard is the LRC Breeders Code of Conduct. It is a requirement for LRC breeder-directory members and a recommendation otherwise. It asks breeders to screen all breeding stock and use only dogs with an OFA CHIC number, to provide pedigree and health reports, to screen buyers, to **provide a written contract** outlining guarantees or restrictions, to insist on right of first refusal if the owner re-homes the dog, and to sell on limited registration or a spay/neuter agreement unless written requirements are met ([LRC Code, PDF](https://thelabradorclub.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/LRC-Breeders-Code-of-Conduct.pdf)). The CHIC minimum for Labs is OFA hips, OFA elbows, an eye exam, EIC DNA and D-locus DNA. prcd-PRA, CNM, cardiac and patella are recommended extras ([LRC Health Statement](https://www.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Labrador-Retriever-Health-Statement.pdf)). Hip and elbow certification needs a dog at least 24 months old, and eye exams must be repeated annually. Buyers can look dogs up on OFA, but abnormal results post only if the owner opted in, so a dog's absence from the database does not prove it is normal ([OFA FAQs](https://ofa.org/about/faqs/)). Note that AKC programs (Bred with H.E.A.R.T., Breeder of Merit) certify health testing and registration, but nothing found says AKC mandates specific website content ([H.E.A.R.T.](https://www.akc.org/breeder-programs/akc-bred-with-heart-program/requirements/); [Breeder of Merit](https://www.akc.org/dog-breeders/breeder-of-merit/requirements/)).

Buyer-facing guides converge on the same checklist. The LRC tells buyers to expect proof of parents' OFA hips, elbows, eyes, EIC and dilute testing, and lists red flags: no clearances, many litters at once, sale before 7 weeks, refusal to show the parents, more interest in a deposit than in your answers, and colors other than black, chocolate and yellow ([LRC Getting a Puppy](https://thelabradorclub.com/getting-a-puppy/)). Humane World and the HSUS brochure add buyer interviews, an in-person visit, and a written requirement to return the dog to the breeder if it cannot be kept ([Humane World](https://www.humaneworld.org/resources/how-find-responsible-dog-breeder); [HSUS](https://m.humanesociety.org/sites/default/files/docs/find-responsible-dog-breeder.pdf)). Good Dog requires take-back and describes socialization from 3 to 13 weeks ([Good Dog](https://www.gooddog.com/what-is-a-good-breeder)). The BBB reports that about 60% of complaints about dog sellers alleged the pet never arrived, and warns about payment by Zelle, CashApp or gift cards and sellers who won't take phone calls ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/all/petscams)). Go-home age is typically 8 weeks; the LRC's red flag is under 7 weeks, and the AKC lemon-law analysis says nearly all states with such laws require 8 (Virginia 7), a figure taken from a search summary and worth verifying ([AKC analysis](https://www.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/ConsumerProtection_Puppy-Lemon-LawsPolicy-Analysis_FINAL.pdf)).

**On the owner's no-contract practice.** This is an inference, not a legal conclusion, and not legal advice. No source directly analyzed no-contract sales. The sources do show that the LRC Code asks for a written contract, limited registration or spay/neuter terms, and right of first refusal, and that buyer guides treat written take-back as a hallmark. A buyer using those checklists may read "no contract" as a red flag. Contract enforcement can be costly for small breeders, and some breeders criticize overly restrictive terms ([AKC on contracts](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/dog-breeding/everything-you-need-to-know-about-breeder-contracts/); [DogNews](https://dognews.com/news/sid-marx-ponders-whether-restrictive-contracts-breeding-terms-do-more-harm-than-good)). Some states' puppy lemon laws may apply regardless of any contract (roughly 22 states as of 2023 per one source), and licensing thresholds vary by state ([Animal Legal Center](https://www.animallaw.info/topic/table-pet-purchaser-protection-acts)). Whether to adopt anything written, and in what form, is the owner's decision, ideally after a local legal check. One lighter option the sources point toward, which the owner may weigh: publish plain-language policies (price, deposit, refund, go-home age, take-back stance) on the site without a long contract. That is not a substitute for legal advice.

Snow Goose's custom admin overlaps most of the baseline already, so buying any of these tools would mean maintaining two systems.

## Marketplaces, builders and the buyer journey: Snow Goose's site can carry what listings can't

Marketplaces demand parent-level facts but little process. An AKC Marketplace listing shows sire and dam with registration numbers, titles, OFA and DNA results, litter date, and the number of males and females available, and only posts after AKC verifies standing ([AKC listing requirements](https://www.apps.akc.org/apps/classified/support/obc_requirements.cfm)). Good Dog screens breeders, awards per-dog health badges and Good/Great/Excellent levels that are self-reported, and requires take-back ([Good Dog standards](https://www.gooddog.com/standards-and-screening)). Hunting Lab Pedigree posts litters free for registered users with price, color and a SOLD OUT status, and deletes silver/dilute listings ([Hunting Lab Pedigree](https://huntinglabpedigree.com/HuntingDogPuppyfinder.asp)). Fees are murky: AKC shows $119/year, $12/month plus $25 setup, or $45 per litter per 90 days on a page that could not be fetched directly, and an older page says $25 per 90 days. Puppies.com is reported at about $30/month from third-party sources ([AKC shop](https://shop.akc.org/products/akc-marketplace-for-breeders); [Citizen Shipper](https://citizenshipper.com/blog/pet-classified-site-profile-puppies-com/)). PuppySpot, Lancaster Puppies and Greenfield carry reputational baggage and have no first-party fee data. The practical point is that a marketplace listing can point back to the kennel's own phone and site, and Snow Goose already has the per-litter facts those listings ask for, apart from registration numbers shown publicly on the litter and OFA links.

Builders explain why stale sites are common. Wix, Squarespace and GoDaddy run about $10-17/month and have no litter, parent or waitlist model ([Appy Pie roundup](https://www.appypie.com/blog/best-website-builder-for-dog-breeders)); breeder-specific Breedpost adds animal profiles, automatic pedigrees and application forms for about **$35/month plus $120 setup** ([Breedpost](https://breedpost.com/blog/dog-breeder-website-checklist-2026-edition)). Vendor guidance converges on a page set: separate current, planned and past litters; a page per sire and dam with health testing; a process/policies page covering application, waitlist order, what the deposit secures, refund terms and go-home age; testimonials; FAQ; an application form; and mobile-friendly design ([Wix](https://www.wix.com/blog/how-to-make-a-dog-breeder-website)). Note that these are vendor marketing pages. Snow Goose has the litters and parent pages; it lacks the process, FAQ, testimonials and application.

The typical buyer journey is: email inquiry, questionnaire, waitlist with deposit, pick order by deposit or breeder matching, weekly photo updates, and go-home at about 8 weeks ([Arctic Texan](https://sites.google.com/site/arctictexanlabradors/our-p/buying-a-puppy)). Deposit and waitlist-fee amounts in the samples ran $50 to $850. Quick replies happen by text and phone, updates run through Facebook groups and video, and the site's job is discovery, health research, application and policy. This is evidence from a few individual breeders. No conversion, response-time or buyer-complaint data was found, so "where buyers are lost" (unanswered inquiries, stale availability) is inference. The implication for Snow Goose is to capture and surface what the owner already does by phone rather than replace it.

## Recommendations ranked for this owner

1. **Add an automatic "last updated" date and a single clear status chip on each litter.** It costs almost nothing, requires no owner effort (the date comes from the admin save), and answers the commonest observed failure. Add an "As of" line only if it is generated, since hand-edited dates went wrong at Heatherdowns.
2. **Upgrade health tests to structured fields with an OFA number and link.** Keep the public/private flag. Add test type (hips, elbows, eyes, EIC, D-locus, CNM, prcd-PRA, cardiac), result, date, and OFA link. Match the LRC minimum list so the dog page reads like the LRC checklist. Show "preliminary" for dogs under 24 months.
3. **Write a one-page "How it works" with a short FAQ and policies section.** Cover price, deposit and what it secures, refund and transfer rules, pick order, 8-week go-home, visit policy, spay/neuter advice, and what happens if a buyer cannot keep the dog. This page also serves as the owner's answer on the no-contract question without forcing a decision. It is mostly text the owner already says on the phone.
4. **Add a public application form that drops into the existing waitlist.** Keep it short (phone-friendly, a few questions about the home and experience, preferred sex and colour), add spam protection, and notify the owner by email or text. Do not publish waitlist names. This replaces the Google Form/Jotform patchwork seen on most sites.
5. **Decide the contract question deliberately.** Options, none a legal recommendation: continue as is and say so plainly; publish a short written health guarantee and take-back policy; or use a one-page bill of sale. If the owner wants any of the second or third, a local attorney or her state's template is the right source. The admin could later store a "terms given" note per puppy.
6. **Add basic SEO and a Google Business Profile.** Page titles, descriptions, a share image, and a phone-friendly contact block. This is cheap, but there is no traffic evidence to size the benefit.
7. **Add a quick puppy weight log designed for phone entry.** Daily or weekly weights per puppy; a small chart on the admin; optionally a "litter diary" of photos and notes on the public page, since weekly updates are what breeders already post on Facebook.
8. **Add a testimonials/"Happy Families" page** (opt-in, first names only) and a "retired dogs" section drawn from the existing role=retired dogs.
9. **Add light reminders and heat tracking, then CSV export.** Reminders for go-home date, vet visit and waitlist follow-up build on the existing dashboard. Heat tracking matters only if she is breeding on a schedule. Export is cheap insurance against lock-in.
10. **Defer pedigrees, expenses, buyer portal, online payments and e-signatures.** They are rare or paid in the market, add support and compliance burden, and do not address the main trust gaps. Revisit if litters grow or buyers ask for them.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the build question. Snow Goose does not need more software; the commercial tools sell record-keeping that the admin already largely has, and charge $9-79/month to add things like portals and payments that a small family kennel doesn't obviously need. The gap is public-facing proof and process: dated status, verifiable health results, plainly stated policies, and an application a buyer can submit. Those are the things the LRC, Good Dog, HSUS and the audited breeder sites all point to, and almost none of the sampled sites do them well.

The unresolved item is the contract question, and the evidence cannot settle it. The sources show that standards bodies and buyer guides expect written terms, but they do not analyze what a no-contract breeder risks, which varies by state. The practical path is to publish clear policies, let the owner decide on written terms with local advice, and treat the rest of the list as incremental, phone-friendly additions. Further evidence worth getting: a UK kennel and a field-trial kennel site, a visual and mobile review of a few top sites, and real breeder or buyer accounts of what causes lost inquiries.
