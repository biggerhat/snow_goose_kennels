# Breeder marketplaces, website builders and the Lab puppy-buyer journey (US, 2026)

Method note: WebFetch hit 429 or 403 on shop.akc.org and saasworthy, and many facts below come through search-result summaries or vendor blogs. Pricing for third-party sites is marked unverified where only secondary sources exist.

## 1. What do marketplaces force breeders to display that a standalone kennel site should also display?

### Takeaway

AKC and Good Dog both make breeders show parent-level health testing and registration or ethics commitments. Marketplace listings are otherwise thin: parents, DOB, counts, contact. The pages that are not on a marketplace (process, deposit, waitlist order, litter status) are what a kennel site can add.

### Cited Findings

- AKC Marketplace/OBC base listing shows breed, breeder name/city/state/zip, phone and email, sire and dam names with AKC reg numbers, titles, OFA and DNA profile, litter DOB, and number of males and females available — [AKC OBC requirements](https://www.apps.akc.org/apps/classified/support/obc_requirements.cfm)
- AKC eligibility: breeder in good standing, litter already AKC-registered, agree to give new owners AKC papers, no brokers/pet shops/auctioneers/flea markets, no litter lot sales; listing posts only after AKC verifies standing — [AKC OBC requirements](https://www.apps.akc.org/apps/classified/support/obc_requirements.cfm)
- Good Dog: breeders apply via questionnaire, are screened (online audit and fraud check possible), agree to community standards and a Breeder Code of Ethics, report on five areas (breeding decisions, physical health, mental health, environment, buyer education/policies), and confirm entry-level breed health testing and vaccination/deworming protocols; puppies stay with litter until typically no earlier than 8 weeks — [Good Dog Standards & Screening](https://www.gooddog.com/standards-and-screening)
- Good Dog badges are per sire and dam when the breeder provides verifiable testing info; programs are rated Good/Great/Excellent from self-reported practice; breeders can link health records on their profile — [Good Dog Standards & Screening](https://www.gooddog.com/standards-and-screening), [What's a Good Breeder](https://www.gooddog.com/what-is-a-good-breeder)
- Good Dog requires breeders to take back and rehome any dog they sold, and to describe socialization in the 3-13 week window — [What's a Good Breeder](https://www.gooddog.com/what-is-a-good-breeder)
- Good Dog follows up with routine check-ins after joining — [Good Dog search summary of standards pages](https://www.gooddog.com/standards-and-screening)
- Hunting Lab Pedigree: litter announcements show location, coat color, asking price, owner contact, and a status such as "SOLD OUT"; users must register; Lab search rules say silver/dilute litters or pedigrees are deleted — [HuntingLabPedigree puppy finder](https://huntinglabpedigree.com/HuntingDogPuppyfinder.asp), example listing [puppy.asp?id=64924](https://www.huntinglabpedigree.com/puppy.asp?id=64924)
- PuppySpot (reported): 100+ point breeder screening, annual review, USDA-licensed broker and requires breeders to be licensed unless exempt, vet evaluation of every placed puppy; secondary/affiliate sources — [Citizen Shipper deep dive](https://citizenshipper.com/blog/a-deep-dive-into-puppyspot/), [PuppySpot breeder screening (pupup help)](https://intercom.help/pupup/en/articles/10229718-breeder-screening-process)
- Greenfield Puppies (reported): partner breeders must provide a 30-day health guarantee; claims of background checks are from review sites and contested by BBB reviewers — [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/pets/greenfield_puppies_pa.html), [BBB](https://prod.bbb.org/us/pa/lampeter/profile/find-a-pet/greenfield-puppies-llc-0241-235979448/customer-reviews)

### Inferences

- A kennel site should at minimum replicate the AKC base listing per litter: sire and dam with reg numbers, titles, OFA/DNA results, DOB, males/females available, plus contact.
- Good Dog's per-dog health-badge model suggests parent pages with linked OFA/CHIC proof are the credibility unit for Labs (EIC, OFA hips/elbows, eyes, etc.; specific Lab test list was not fetched).
- Pricing and "SOLD OUT" status are normal on Lab-specific directories, so showing price and status is not unusual.

### Gaps

- Lancaster Puppies, Greenfield, PuppySpot and Good Dog official breeder-requirement pages were not retrievable; no first-party text found.
- "Nextdaypets" returned no results; could not verify it exists as described.
- Good Dog's exact Lab entry-level test list not fetched.

## 2. What fees/commissions do marketplaces charge, and why might a kennel prefer its own site?

### Takeaway

Fees range from free (Hunting Lab Pedigree posting) to about $12-$119 for AKC and roughly $30/month for Puppies.com; Good Dog, PuppySpot and Lancaster/Greenfield costs are not verifiable from first-party sources. Reasons to own a site: control of the buyer relationship, no per-listing renewals, and showing process/waitlist that listings cannot.

### Cited Findings

- AKC Marketplace shop page (via search summary): annual plan $119/year, monthly $12/month plus $25 sign-up, single litter $45/90 days; Breeder of Merit gets free litter listings and prime placement — [AKC shop](https://shop.akc.org/products/akc-marketplace-for-breeders), [BoM benefits](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/dog-breeding/akc-marketplace-benefits-for-breeders-of-merit/)
- Conflicting: the older OBC requirements page says $25 per 90 days, refundable only if litter unregisterable — [AKC OBC requirements](https://www.apps.akc.org/apps/classified/support/obc_requirements.cfm). The shop page is newer, but I could not fetch it directly (429).
- Puppies.com (formerly PuppyFind, rebranded 2022 per one source): free 3 days then paid; $29.99/month, $79.99/3 months, $199.99/year; caps of 20 (shorter plans) to 40 listings; annual auto-renews; sources disagree on prices (one says $29.95) — [Citizen Shipper on Puppies.com](https://citizenshipper.com/blog/pet-classified-site-profile-puppies-com/), [SideHusl](https://sidehusl.com/puppyfind/). Unverified, third-party.
- Hunting Lab Pedigree: adding a litter is free for registered users; an "Advertise" option exists (terms not opened) — [HuntingLabPedigree](https://huntinglabpedigree.com/HuntingDogPuppyfinder.asp)
- Good Dog: no breeder listing fee published on pages found. Buyer-side: ACH free, card processing fee, optional "Protection & Support" fee — [Good Dog Trustpilot](https://nl.trustpilot.com/review/gooddog.com) (reviews, company replies quoted in search summary)
- PuppySpot: one source says no fees for breeders, buyers pay; critic says breeders get a fraction of price and contact info is hidden from buyers — [SideHusl PuppySpot](https://sidehusl.com/puppyspot/), [Citizen Shipper](https://citizenshipper.com/blog/a-deep-dive-into-puppyspot/). Unverified/conflicting.
- Lancaster Puppies: one ResellerRatings review mentions $15 per puppy; no documented fee schedule found — [ResellerRatings](https://www.resellerratings.com/amp/store/Lancasterpuppies). Anecdote only.
- Lancaster Puppies is described as a classified site with no guarantee; anyone can list, so mills may appear — [Review42](https://review42.com/reviews/pet-insurance/lancaster-puppies). Brokers in Lancaster County have been criticized by PETA over "adoption" marketing — [PETA](https://www.peta.org/media/news-releases/lancaster-co-dog-brokers-hit-with-cease-and-desist-from-peta-over-false-adoption-marketing/)

### Inferences

- For a Lab hobby breeder selling a few litters, AKC single-litter or Hunting Lab Pedigree free posting is cheap; a standalone site (see section 3, about $20-$35/month) costs similar to one Puppies.com month and carries the whole relationship.
- Brokered or high-volume marketplaces (PuppySpot, Lancaster, Greenfield) carry reputational baggage that a reputable Lab hunting/field kennel may not want to be associated with; this is my inference, not a sourced preference.
- A marketplace listing should point back to the kennel site/phone for the application and waitlist, since AKC lets breeder phone/email show.

### Gaps

- No first-party fee tables for Puppies.com, Good Dog, PuppySpot, Lancaster Puppies, Greenfield, Nextdaypets.
- Commission percentages for brokered sites not found.
- Facebook group rules (e.g., Lab-specific buy/sell groups) and Instagram policies not researched.

## 3. Website builders: features, prices, common problems

### Takeaway

General builders (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy, Google Sites) are cheap and widely used by Lab breeders but have no litter, parent or waitlist model; breeder-specific builders (Breedpost) add those for about $35/month. The most-cited failure is stale litter information.

### Cited Findings

- Price ranges (general reviews, promotional pricing varies): Wix from about $17/month; Squarespace about $16/month; GoDaddy about $9.99/month, with a free plan mentioned — [Appy Pie roundup](https://www.appypie.com/blog/best-website-builder-for-dog-breeders), [Shopify cheap builders](https://www.shopify.com/blog/cheap-website-builder). Not breeder-specific; confirm on vendor sites.
- Wix's own breeder guide recommends: homepage with call to action; separate current, planned and past litter pages; one page per sire/dam with health testing; process/policies page covering how to apply, waitlist order, what deposit secures, refund terms, go-home age; About; testimonials; contact form/phone/social; FAQ; mobile-friendly design; online application form; newsletter; SSL; Google Business Profile — [Wix blog](https://www.wix.com/blog/how-to-make-a-dog-breeder-website) (vendor marketing)
- Breedpost (breeder-specific builder): profile page per animal, automatic pedigrees, breeding pages, application forms/waitlist signups/enquiries, content updates without design work, news posts linked to animals; $120 one-off setup, $35/month or $348/year — [Breedpost checklist 2026](https://breedpost.com/blog/dog-breeder-website-checklist-2026-edition) (vendor page)
- Breedpost advises keeping available-stock status updated, listing outdated litter info as a mistake — [Breedpost](https://breedpost.com/blog/dog-breeder-website-checklist-2026-edition)
- Breederspot: listed in SaaSworthy but page returned 403; pricing not verified — [SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/breederspot/pricing)
- Common problems: sites as "digital graveyards" with no updates, dated design, desktop-first layouts, heavy images and slow pages on mobile, autoplay music — [Breeding Business](https://breedingbusiness.com/mistakes-kennel-websites/) (consultant blog)
- A Basenji forum thread remarks a site not updated in years is of little use even for a responsible breeder — [Basenji Forums](https://basenjiforums.com/topic/11941/sharing-websites-how-to-tell-the-difference/6)
- Example Lab/breeder sites on these tools: Google Sites (Arctic Texan Labradors, [buying a puppy](https://sites.google.com/site/arctictexanlabradors/our-p/buying-a-puppy)), Squarespace templates ([example](https://azalea-chimes-wlge.squarespace.com/Purchasing)), forms via Jotform ([example questionnaire](https://form.jotform.com/westlanedogs/westlanes-questionnaire)).

### Inferences

- Breeders commonly bolt a Jotform or Google Form onto a generic site for the application; a site with a built-in application and litter status removes that patchwork.
- Because Wix/Squarespace/Google Sites need manual edits per litter, stale pages are structural, not laziness.

### Gaps

- No Capterra/Trustpilot reviews of Squarespace/Wix/GoDaddy for breeders found; no data on WordPress breeder themes, BreederWebsites or Kennel-site vendors; Google Sites price (free with Google account) not cited.
- No quantitative data on mobile share of breeder-site traffic (the Breeding Business claim is unsourced).

## 4. Buyer journey, expectations, and where buyers are lost

### Takeaway

Typical flow is email inquiry, questionnaire, waitlist with deposit, pick order by deposit or breeder matching, weekly photo/video updates, 8-week go-home. Application, policies and contract belong on the website; updates and chatter happen in Facebook groups, text and video calls.

### Cited Findings

- Arctic Texan Labradors (Google Sites, service-dog lines): email interest, questionnaire, references, video interview, contract, waitlist; $100 refundable registration fee credited to price; $400 non-refundable deposit at birth reservation; early updates by video calls and private Facebook group; meet puppies at 6-7 weeks; pickup in person, pups often stay to 9-10 weeks; Limited AKC registration, spay/neuter contract, vet-checked and microchipped; email is main channel — [Arctic Texan Labradors](https://sites.google.com/site/arctictexanlabradors/our-p/buying-a-puppy)
- Other breeder forms: $50 non-refundable waitlist fee credited to price; $500-$850 non-refundable deposits; selection in deposit order, sometimes after temperament testing around 7 weeks; "pick by 6 weeks"; 8 weeks standard go-home (PA law cited); balance due at pickup with a sales contract; vaccinations and deworming included — [search summary of breeder pages](https://sites.google.com/site/arctictexanlabradors/our-p/buying-a-puppy), [Apple Valley Labradors reservations](https://sites.google.com/view/applevalleylabradors.ca/applevalleylabrador/home/puppies/reservations) (note: that one is Canadian; deposits vary, non-US results possible)
- Breeders send weekly photos from birth to 8 weeks, create per-litter Facebook groups, post on Instagram, and text photos/videos — [Arctic Texan Labradors home](https://sites.google.com/site/arctictexanlabradors), [Jotform breeder form](https://form.jotform.com/93347270829162)
- Good Dog tells buyers to ask about health testing, temperament, training, socialization and enrichment, and how the breeder matches puppies to families, and to talk to breeders directly — [What's a Good Breeder](https://www.gooddog.com/what-is-a-good-breeder)
- AKC notes high demand means breeders need little marketing, but a good website helps screen buyers — [AKC website article](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/dog-breeding/make-impact-creating-best-website/) (summarized via search; not fetched)
- Wix guide: process/policies page should explain application, waitlist order, deposit meaning, refund terms and go-home timing, linked from every litter page — [Wix blog](https://www.wix.com/blog/how-to-make-a-dog-breeder-website)
- Weekly posts, photos and a social feed are recommended to avoid looking absent — [Breeding Business](https://breedingbusiness.com/mistakes-kennel-websites/)

### Inferences

- Lowest-effort high-value features: a status banner on each litter (planned / expecting / born / all reserved) with date; a "How it works" page (application, deposit, order, 8-week go-home); a FAQ; parent pages with health test links; a weekly-photos "litter diary"; an embedded or linked application form; a social-feed or Facebook link. These match what breeders already do by text/Facebook, so the site should capture and surface it, not replace it.
- Touchpoints on the website: discover, research health tests, application, policies/contract/price. Touchpoints off-site: quick replies by text/phone, video calls, private Facebook group updates, pickup coordination.
- Likely buyer loss points: unanswered inquiry and stale "available" pages; evidence is thin (see gaps) and these are inferences.

### Gaps

- No quantitative response-time or conversion data from buyers or breeders found; no Reddit or forum threads fetched on Lab buyer complaints.
- Lab-specific buyer-guide content (e.g., Labrador Retriever Club, hunting-lab buyers' priorities) not retrieved.
- Facebook/Instagram usage evidence is limited to a handful of individual breeder sites.
