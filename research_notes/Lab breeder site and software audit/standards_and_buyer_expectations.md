# Standards and Buyer Expectations for Responsible Labrador Breeders (US, as of late 2026)

Note on method: several pages were read through a summarizing fetch tool, and some search-derived items are second-hand; those are flagged. The coordinator's limit of ~15 tool calls was reached, so several sub-topics are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Official standards: AKC programs and the Labrador Retriever Club (LRC) Code

### Takeaway

The LRC (AKC parent club) Breeders Code of Conduct is the most Lab-specific and explicit standard: CHIC-number-backed health screening, buyer screening, written contract, right of first refusal, and limited registration or spay/neuter for pet puppies. AKC Bred with H.E.A.R.T. and Breeder of Merit are certification-style programs (health testing per parent club, registration, education), not rules about what must appear on a website.

### Cited Findings

- LRC Breeders Code of Conduct (10 points; REQUIREMENT for LRC breeders-directory members, otherwise a recommendation): breeder involved in the breed at least 5 years and belongs to at least one other AKC club; must "offer information on health issues" (hip and elbow dysplasia, seizures, heart disease, neuromuscular disorders, rare aggression); screen all breeding stock and use only dogs with an OFA CHIC number (hips/elbows cleared at 24+ months; eye clearance within one year of breeding; EIC DNA; D-locus dilute DNA); annual ACVO eye exam recommended for breeding stock; EIC need not be "clear" to breed (testing is the requirement); CNM, PRCD and cardiac screening "highly recommended"; breeder "shall provide pedigree information and health reports confirming the hip, eye, and elbow status"; "shall screen all buyers for their ability and intent to properly care for a dog"; "shall provide a written contract outlining the terms and duration of any guarantees or restrictions"; "shall insist on the right of first refusal if the original owner wishes to re-home the dog"; "shall define the use of Limited Registration and sell all puppies on limited registration or a spay/neuter agreement unless specific written requirements are met." — [LRC Breeders Code of Conduct (PDF)](https://thelabradorclub.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/LRC-Breeders-Code-of-Conduct.pdf)
- LRC Breeders Directory: applicant must be an LRC member in good standing 2+ years and abide by the Code of Conduct and LRC Constitution goals. — [same PDF](https://thelabradorclub.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/LRC-Breeders-Code-of-Conduct.pdf)
- LRC "Getting a puppy" page (buyer-facing; via summarizing fetch): expect proof of parents' OFA hips, elbows, eyes, EIC, and dilute (dd) testing; buyers can look parents up on ofa.org; breeder should provide vaccination and deworming records; ask about health guarantee; vet exam soon after pickup. Red flags listed: no parent clearances; multiple breeds or many litters at once; no AKC pedigree on request; puppies sold before 7 weeks; refusal to show facility or parents (sire may be off-site); more interest in a deposit than in your answers; colors other than black, chocolate, yellow (LRC treats others, incl. dilutes, as disqualifying). — [LRC Getting a Puppy](https://thelabradorclub.com/getting-a-puppy/)
- LRC health statement (Jan 21, 2021; board vote June 16, 2016): CHIC requirements = OFA hip, OFA elbow, OFA eye, EIC, D-locus DNA. Recommended additional: prcd-PRA, CNM, cardiac exam (including Advanced Cardiac for many dogs), patella exam; many members also test thyroid. Permanent ID (microchip/tattoo) required at time of testing. — [AKC-hosted LRC Health Statement (PDF)](https://www.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Labrador-Retriever-Health-Statement.pdf)
- AKC Bred with H.E.A.R.T. requirements: at least one AKC litter registered in past 5 years; good standing; certify the parent-club-recommended health screens are performed on breeding stock and be ready to show proof on request; agree to AKC Care and Conditions Policy including inspections; comply with federal/state/local dog laws; complete AKC-approved/provided breeder education each year; online application. — [AKC H.E.A.R.T. requirements](https://www.akc.org/breeder-programs/akc-bred-with-heart-program/requirements/) (search summary indicates the program is free to join; an older press release cited a $25 fee: [AKC H.E.A.R.T. program](https://www.akc.org/breeder-programs/akc-bred-with-heart-program/))
- AKC Breeder of Merit base requirements: 5+ years involved in AKC events; commit to individually registering 100% of puppies; titles on at least 4 dogs from AKC litters bred/co-bred per breed; membership in an AKC breed club; certification that parent-club health tests are done. Advanced level: proof of parent-club-required testing on sires and dams. Recognition tiers Bronze (10 titled dogs) through Platinum (100). The page does not say what counts as proof. — [AKC Breeder of Merit requirements](https://www.akc.org/dog-breeders/breeder-of-merit/requirements/)
- AKC's breed health testing page lists the Lab parent club's recommended tests: hip, elbow, ophthalmologist evaluation, D-locus, CNM, PRA-prcd, EIC. — [AKC Sporting Group Health Testing Requirements](https://www.akc.org/breeder-programs/breed-health-testing-requirements/sporting-group-health-testing-requirements/)
- AKC Marketplace (search summary, conflicting/older sources): listings only for litters already AKC-registered; AKC verifies standing before posting; no litter "lot" sales; listing may be removed for inaccurate reporting; falsely advertising can mean suspension of AKC privileges; a 2018 AKC document tells Breeder of Merit members not to advertise on Marketplace without proof of required testing. Pricing seen on AKC shop page: $119/yr, $12/mo + $25 setup, $45 per litter/90 days (verify current). — [Marketplace listing requirements (older OBC page)](https://www.apps.akc.org/apps/classified/support/obc_requirements.cfm); [AKC Marketplace for Breeders](https://shop.akc.org/products/akc-marketplace-for-breeders); [BoM health testing note 2018](https://s3.amazonaws.com/cdn-origin-etr.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/31133242/Important-Info-Regarding-Health-Testing-Breeder-of-Merit.pdf)
- Good Dog (private platform) screens breeders across breeding practices, physical health, mental health, housing, and buyer education/policies; requires breed-specific testing; awards Good/Great/Excellent health-testing levels based on self-report; breeders can link health info for each dog; must take back and rehome dogs if needed; describe socialization (3-13 weeks) and vet care. — [Good Dog: What's a Good Breeder](https://www.gooddog.com/what-is-a-good-breeder); levels are self-reported per search summary of [Good Dog health testing levels](https://www.gooddog.com/guide-to-good-dogs-health-testing-levels)

### Inferences

- Nothing found says AKC mandates specific website content; the nearest "publish/provide" requirements are LRC point 4 (offer health information), point 6 (provide pedigree and hip/elbow/eye reports), and H.E.A.R.T./BoM "proof on request". A site that displays results with OFA links meets these in spirit.
- LRC point 10 (limited registration or spay/neuter agreement) plus point 8 (written contract) means a "no contract" policy departs from the LRC Code (see section 3).

- Current AKC Marketplace page rules and fees not verified from a primary current page.
- LRC "code of ethics" (separate from the Breeders Code of Conduct) not retrieved; the Conduct PDF above is the one found.

## 2. Lab health testing: minimum vs nice-to-have, CHIC, and verification

### Takeaway

Per the LRC, the CHIC minimum for Labs is OFA hips, OFA elbows, OFA/ACVO eye exam, EIC DNA, and D-locus DNA; prcd-PRA, CNM, cardiac, patella (and thyroid) are recommended extras. Buyers verify via OFA's public search by OFA/CHIC number, but abnormal results may not be public.

### Cited Findings

- Minimum (CHIC-required for Labs): hips, elbows, eye exam, EIC, D-locus. — [LRC Health Statement](https://www.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Labrador-Retriever-Health-Statement.pdf); [LRC Code](https://thelabradorclub.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/LRC-Breeders-Code-of-Conduct.pdf)
- Nice-to-have per LRC: prcd-PRA, CNM, cardiac (Basic vs Advanced; Advanced needs a cardiologist), patella; thyroid by many members. CNM described as optional but encouraged. — same LRC statement; glossary at [LRC health page](https://thelabradorclub.com/health/) defines Basic/Advanced cardiac, SD2 and HNPK, but the page content retrieved gave no recommendation about SD2/HNPK.
- AKC lists D-locus, CNM, PRA-prcd and EIC DNA along with hip, elbow, ophthalmologist as parent-club recommended. — [AKC](https://www.akc.org/breeder-programs/breed-health-testing-requirements/sporting-group-health-testing-requirements/)
- Eye clearance must be by a board-certified ACVO ophthalmologist (search summary of LRC health page); an LRC Code requires eye clearance within 1 year of breeding. — [LRC health](https://thelabradorclub.com/health/); [LRC Code](https://thelabradorclub.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/LRC-Breeders-Code-of-Conduct.pdf)
- Search summary says D-locus became a CHIC requirement 11/1/16 (single search-snippet source; verify). — [LRC health](https://thelabradorclub.com/health/)
- Permanent ID (microchip/tattoo) required for CHIC and for the dog at testing. — [LRC statement](https://www.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Labrador-Retriever-Health-Statement.pdf)
- Verification: OFA "Search OFA Health-Tested Dogs"; results appear about 1 week after evaluation; OFA numbers (e.g. LR-100E24M-C-PI) encode breed, grade, age in months, sex, ID status; suffix PI = permanent ID, VPI = vet-verified ID, NOPI = none; hips normal grades Excellent/Good/Fair; certification requires 24+ months (younger = preliminary only); normal results for dogs over 24 months post by default, abnormal results only if owner opted in, so absence from the database does not prove normal. — [OFA FAQs](https://ofa.org/about/faqs/)
- Elbow radiograph must be the extreme-flexed medial-lateral view (search summary of OFA). — [OFA eye/FAQ pages](https://ofa.org/diseases/eye-disease/eye-faqs/)

### Inferences

- Display per dog (sire and dam): hip grade+OFA number, elbow grade+number, eye exam date+number, EIC/D-locus/CNM/prcd genotype+OFA number, cardiac if done, CHIC number, each linking to the OFA record; show test date and dog's age at testing (eye exams expire annually).
- Showing "carrier"/"at-risk" genotypes openly is consistent with the LRC allowing EIC non-clear dogs to be bred; hiding them is the risk, since OFA abnormal results may be unlisted.

### Gaps

- Primary CHIC Labrador page (ofa.org) returned 404; CHIC number mechanics taken from LRC documents only.
- No source found on SD2 or HNPK as recommendations for Labs (both are Lab-linked conditions per the LRC glossary only).
- Penn HIP/Embark acceptance not researched.

## 3. Breeder-ethics and buyer-guide sources: what a trustworthy breeder discloses

### Takeaway

Humane World, Good Dog, and the LRC converge on: buyer screening, visits to see parents and housing, written contract with spay/neuter or limited registration, lifetime take-back, vet records, health clearances. Pricing and deposit norms are not set by any authoritative source found; breeder-published policies vary widely. No source endorses "no contract" sales; LRC explicitly requires a written contract.

### Cited Findings

- Humane World (HSUS): responsible breeder interviews you, does not sell through pet stores or websites or "to the first buyer with cash", expects in-person visit to see where puppies are born/raised; referrals from vets, breed clubs, shows. — [Humane World](https://www.humaneworld.org/resources/how-find-responsible-dog-breeder)
- Humane World/HSUS brochure (search summary; quotes): breeder will typically require in writing spay/neuter and return of the dog to the breeder if you cannot keep it at any point; has a vet relationship and can show individual vet records for puppy and parents; meeting at neutral location or shipping unseen is a red flag. — [HSUS find-responsible-breeder PDF](https://m.humanesociety.org/sites/default/files/docs/find-responsible-dog-breeder.pdf)
- BBB: about 60% of reports regarding dog sellers alleged the pet never arrived; warning signs: price too good to be true, seller won't take phone calls, seller dictates shipper, payment via Zelle/CashApp/gift cards, never seeing the pet; tip: reverse image search photos. — [BBB pet scams](https://www.bbb.org/all/petscams); [BBB puppy scam update](https://www.bbb.org/all/scamstudies/puppy-scams/puppy-scam-study-update)
- Deposits/waitlists (breeder samples, not standards): refundable vs non-refundable varies; pick order by deposit date; refund only if breeder cannot supply a puppy or puppy is ill; balance due before pickup; release around 8 weeks. — [sample breeder policy, Stokeshire](https://stokeshire-designer-doodles.squarespace.com/returns-policy); [other samples via search](https://form.jotform.com/202015454879155)
- Contracts: AKC says limited registration and/or spay/neuter commonly addressed in contract; contract provisions are generally enforceable but litigation cost is prohibitive for breeders (secondary summary). — [AKC puppy contract](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/dog-breeding/whats-puppy-contract/); [AKC breeder contracts](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/dog-breeding/everything-you-need-to-know-about-breeder-contracts/)
- Counter-argument seen: some breeders criticize overly restrictive contract terms (multi-generation spay/neuter) — [DogNews](https://dognews.com/news/sid-marx-ponders-whether-restrictive-contracts-breeding-terms-do-more-harm-than-good)
- Go-home age: LRC red flag is sale before 7 weeks; state puppy-law summaries say nearly all states with such laws require 8 weeks (Virginia 7). — [LRC](https://thelabradorclub.com/getting-a-puppy/); [AKC puppy lemon law analysis](https://www.akc.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/ConsumerProtection_Puppy-Lemon-LawsPolicy-Analysis_FINAL.pdf) (figure from search summary; verify)

### Inferences

- A breeder dropping contracts risks: departing from the LRC Code (point 8, 9, 10) and thereby buyers who use LRC/Good Dog/HSUS checklists will read "no contract" as a red flag; no written health guarantee or take-back; no way to restrict breeding or secure first refusal; in lemon-law states, statutory disclosure/remedies may apply regardless. A defensible alternative to a long contract: a short bill of sale plus health guarantee, limited registration, and written take-back clause. (This is inference, not legal advice; the sources did not directly analyze no-contract risk.)
- Publishing price, deposit amount, refund terms and go-home age answers the common "deposit-first" red flag and the BBB too-cheap/payment-method signals.

### Gaps

- ASPCA guidance not retrieved. HSUS checklist page ("How to Identify Responsible Dog Breeders") not read directly.
- No authoritative source on whether to publish prices; no study found on buyer preference for visible pricing.
- Microchip and 8-week vaccine/deworm/vet-check expectations are covered only by LRC (vaccination/deworming records) and generic state-law summaries; no dedicated source on microchip as a standard.

## 4. Buyer-side red flags and trust signals; legal/regulatory notes for a small home breeder site

### Takeaway

Buyers and guides cite: visible parent health records with OFA links, ability to visit and meet the dam, questions asked of the buyer, consistent phone communication, no pressure for deposits, and no payment-app-only or ship-unseen deals. Legal points are state-specific; licensing thresholds and "puppy lemon" disclosure laws vary and should be checked per state.

### Cited Findings

- Red flags: no clearances; multiple breeds or many litters; refusal to show facility/parents; deposit-first; early sale; non-standard colors sold as rare — [LRC](https://thelabradorclub.com/getting-a-puppy/). Neutral-location meets, shipping without a visit, no buyer questions — [HSUS](https://m.humanesociety.org/sites/default/files/docs/find-responsible-dog-breeder.pdf). Scam patterns (stolen photos, unreachable seller, shipper chosen by seller, payment apps) — [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/all/petscams).
- AKC registration alone does not guarantee ethical breeding (search-result note on a third-party site) — [Rover](https://www.rover.com/blog/how-do-you-spot-puppy-mill).
- Licensing thresholds vary: Minnesota (10+ intact adults and more than 5 litters/yr), Connecticut (more than 2 litters), Vermont (3+ litters), New York (9+ sold, exemption under 25/yr from home), Texas (5+ intact adult females); California AB 1482 and other bills pending/possibly changed. — [Animal Legal and Historical Center table](https://www.animallaw.info/topic/table-state-commercial-pet-breeders-laws); [MN BAH](https://bah.state.mn.us/commercial-dog-and-cat-breeder-do-you-need-a-license); [NY Ag](https://agriculture.ny.gov/become-pet-dealer); [TX TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/media/pdf/BRE-at-a-Glance.pdf); [AKC state issues May 2025](https://www.akc.org/clubs-delegates/government-relations/newsletters/taking-command/state-issues-may-2025/). Thresholds are from search summaries; verify each.
- Puppy "lemon laws": roughly 22 states per Animal Legal Center (as of 2023; other counts differ); typically require disclosure of health info, remedies (refund/exchange/vet reimbursement), often non-waivable; many require disclosing breeder license number, and a minimum sale age of 8 weeks. — [Animal Legal Center](https://www.animallaw.info/topic/table-pet-purchaser-protection-acts); [AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/dog-breeding/puppy-lemon-laws/)

### Inferences

- A kennel site should state: state licensing status or exemption (and license number where required), litter count per year, ability to visit/meet dam, no shipping-unseen policy (or video/visit steps), accepted payment methods, real photos of own dogs, and contact by phone.

- Sales-tax treatment of puppy sales and FTC advertising rules not researched; no source retrieved. Pending bills (NY, CA, FL) not verified.
